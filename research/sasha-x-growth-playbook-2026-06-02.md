## Key Findings

### 1. The 2026 X Algorithm: What Actually Matters

**Finding:** The algorithm as of January 2026 uses a Grok-powered transformer model that reads post semantics and watches video. It replaced the prior engagement-count-plus-social-graph system.

**Evidence (Grok live search, citing opentweet.io/blog, teract.ai, sproutsocial.com, conbersa.ai):**

- **Replies that spawn further replies** are the highest-weighted signal. Various reports put this at 13.5x-27x a like; a sustained two-way conversation can reach 75x. The first 30-60 minutes of engagement velocity determines whether a post breaks into broader For You distribution.
- **Bookmarks** signal approximately 10x a like (private action, strong relevance signal).
- **Dwell time** adds ~+10 weight points in some reported models. Staying on a tweet more than two minutes in a conversation boosts ranking. Video watch time beyond 10 seconds adds further lift.
- **Follows-per-impression** compounds over time but is secondary for initial ranking. Small accounts with a high engagement rate per post can outperform larger accounts.
- **External links in the post body** suppress reach by approximately 50% in creator tests. Links go in the first reply.
- **Hashtags** have minimal impact. The semantic NLP model now classifies topic by content, not tags.
- **Native threads (3+ tweets)** often outperform single posts by 3x in impressions.
- **Premium (X Blue)** gives a real visibility lift in conversations and For You. Not documented as a massive multiplier, but consistent across reports.

**What changed from 2025 to 2026:**
- Pre-2026 system weighted raw engagement counts. The current system weights understood quality (original insights over templates) and conversation loops.
- X has cracked down on reward-app farming (Kaito/Cookie Yaps programs ended after API restrictions). Coordinated reply farming is more actively filtered.
- "Iron Slopdome" framing from X leadership (Nikita Bier) has made CT more sensitive to AI-slop templates. Generic AI-generated content now triggers suppression signals.

**Confidence: High.** Multiple independent sources converge. Exact numeric weights are from creator tests and open-source code, not official documentation — treat as directional.

### 2. CT-Native Growth for Small Accounts

**Finding:** The reply game remains the primary growth mechanic in 2026, but it has tightened. Quality and timing beat volume.

**Evidence (Grok live, Perplexity synthesis, citing teract.ai, tweetfull, opentweet.io, creator case studies):**

**What works:**
- Reply within 5-30 minutes of a target post going live. Teract analyzed 300 accounts that grew from under 1k to 10k+ in Q1 2026; 84% used strategic replies as the primary lever and emphasized early-window timing.
- Target accounts 5-20x your size (10k-200k range), not only mega accounts. Replies on accounts with 100k+ often get buried; replies on 15k-50k accounts in a hot thread get seen.
- 15-25 high-quality replies per day. The documented floor from multiple sources.
- A reply must add something the original didn't: a new data point, a contrarian angle, a specific follow-up question. First line of the reply should stand alone in thread preview.
- Narrative-surfing: time entry into trending CT threads (new protocol launches, agent meta discussions) with unique angles. Early, high-signal replies get amplified; late me-too posts are invisible.

**What does not work (and risks a ban):**
- Generic affirmations ("great thread", "this", "ser"). Actively filtered.
- Wallet addresses, ticker spam, self-promotional links in replies.
- High-volume AI-generated replies that pattern-match to templates. X has targeted this specifically post-2026 updates.
- Engagement pods and raids: coordinated low-quality activity now triggers spam filters more aggressively. Small, genuine mutual-support groups still exist, but formal pods at scale are devalued.

**Quote-tweet strategy:** QRTs with strong original narrative extensions work if they drive conversation. But original posts are favored over QRTs by the algorithm. Use QRTs sparingly and only when you have a genuinely different angle.

**Getting amplified by bigger accounts:** The primary path is a reply that stands out enough for the original poster to reply to, like, or repost. No shortcut. Build a recognizable voice first; amplification follows from consistency.

**Confidence: High.** Convergent across Grok's live X data and Perplexity's synthesis of 2025-2026 creator guides.

### 3. Outside-CT Creator Growth (0 to 1k)

**Finding:** The reply-guy method, niche specificity, and build-in-public content all still work in 2026. Text-first posts outperform video for engagement rate on X.

**Evidence (Perplexity, citing teract.ai, sproutsocial.com, tweetfull, r/microsaas, adilo.com):**

**Reply-guy documented cases:**
- A 2026 r/microsaas post documented 0 to ~1,500 followers in 50 days using 20-30 replies per day plus 1-2 originals daily. ~1M impressions in the first 30 days. Niche: micro-SaaS and indie tools.
- Graham Mann's 2026 article gives a trajectory of 100-300 followers in Month 1, 300-1,000 in Months 2-3, driven by 30 minutes per day of replies to larger accounts.
- TweetFull's 2026 strategy: "most new accounts posting daily and engaging strategically can see 500-1,000 targeted followers within 60-90 days."

**Content format data:**
- Adilo's 2025 X engagement study: text-only posts averaged 3.2% engagement rate, images 2.09%, video 1.34%. Median engagement: text 1.13%, video 0.94%.
- Sprout Social 2026: 37% of X users prefer short-form video, 36% prefer text. Aggregate engagement rates still favor text slightly (0.48% vs. 0.41%).
- Implication: for a niche expert account, **high-signal text and threads are the best cost-benefit play.** Video adds differentiation only when execution is strong.

**Build-in-public:**
- Still works when niche and data-rich. Generic "I'm hustling" content is saturated.
- Winning post formats: "Builder log: [specific metric moved from X to Y]." Monthly metrics threads with screenshots. Postmortem threads on failures.
- Consistent posting cadence matters more than volume: one substantial post per day beats five low-signal posts.

**Niche-down:**
- Narrower niches grow faster early. X's topic graph classifies you more clearly when you stay in one domain. Users are more likely to follow when your profile promises a specific type of value.
- Effective bio formula from 2026 guides: "I [action] [very specific subject] [specific mechanism]."
- For the first 90 days: 2-3 content pillars maximum.

**Collaboration ladder (no payment required):**
- Days 1-30: Be a regular in Spaces hosted by mid-tier accounts in your niche. Ask specific questions. Tweet summaries afterward.
- Days 30-60: DM a host with a specific co-hosting pitch on a narrow topic.
- Ongoing: Quote-tweet "mini case studies" building on bigger accounts' posts. One per week.
- Monthly Spaces co-hosted with a complementary account. Turn each Space into a summary thread for persistent content.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** Creator case studies are detailed but not peer-reviewed. Treat timelines as directional.

## What to STOP Doing

**Stop #1: Posting with links in the tweet body.**
Every post that contains a URL in the body is taking a ~50% reach penalty. This is a structural mistake. Fix before anything else.

**Stop #2: Posting originals on a fixed schedule without timing replies to the target accounts' posting schedule.**
The fixed Buffer schedule for originals is fine. The problem is the reply engine does not appear to be calibrated to the 5-30 minute golden window. Replies posted hours after target posts are near-invisible.

**Stop #3: Below-threshold reply volume (6/day when 15-25 is the floor).**
At 6 replies per day, the account is not generating enough signal for the algorithm to test distribution. It is the equivalent of whispering in a loud room.

**Stop #4: Replies that do not clearly originate from Sasha's specific position.**
A reply that could have been written by any CT account adds no voice equity. Every reply should be legible as coming from "an AI agent with a real wallet and real onchain history." This is the one differentiator that generic accounts cannot fake.

**Stop #5: Treating the podcast as a separate asset from the X growth engine.**
Token Trends episodes are the highest-signal raw material Sasha has. Every episode should generate at least one X hook (a data point, a finding, a counter-argument from the recording) that feeds both a thread and reply material for the week. The podcast and X should be one integrated content loop, not parallel tracks.
